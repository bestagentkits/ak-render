# Responsive layout notes

Branch `gh10/wp2-layout`.

## Changelog lines

- feat(layout): A `grid-item` places its blocks across a 12-track grid with `span`, `tabletSpan`, `mobileSpan`, `rowSpan`, `start` and `align`. A grid that holds one switches to 12 tracks, and its other children keep the width they had (12 / `columns`).
- feat(layout): `grid.columns` accepts 1–12 or `auto`. With `auto`, `minItemWidth` (`narrow`, `medium` or `wide`) sets the smallest card width. Grids also take `gap` and `align`.
- feat(layout): `stack.align` places children at the start, center or end instead of stretching them.
- feat(layout): Three semantic page layouts:
  - `sidebar-layout`: a `sidebar` column (an `<aside>`) beside the main `blocks`, on either `side`;
  - `main-aside`: an `aside` column that stays in view on wide screens;
  - `rail-layout`: a compact `rail`. A rail of only links or toolbars is a labelled `<nav>`, and it becomes a strip above the content on narrow screens.

## DESIGN notes (to fold into DESIGN.md, "Responsive")

- **Tracked grids.** A grid with a `grid-item` gets `data-ak-tracks="12"`. Placement is static attribute CSS (`data-ak-span`, `data-ak-tablet-span`, and so on), generated by loops over fixed ranges in `src/blocks/layout/layout-styles.ts`. Nothing is inline, because the CSP forbids it.
  - Wide screens: spans, `start` and `rowSpan` apply.
  - ≤768px: `tabletSpan` applies, otherwise the span stays. `start` is dropped. Plain children pair up (span 6), as in an untracked grid.
  - ≤480px: everything is full width unless `mobileSpan` is set, and `rowSpan` is dropped.
  - The base `≤480` rule `.ak-grid{grid-template-columns:1fr!important}` is unchanged. The tracked grid keeps its 12 tracks through a more specific `!important` rule in its feature sheet.
- **Columns 7–12** drop to 2 columns at ≤768px and to 1 at ≤480px, like 1–6. **`columns: auto`** uses `repeat(auto-fit,minmax(min(100%,<width>),1fr))` at every width, and collapses to one column at ≤480px.
- **Semantic layouts** are one column below 769px, in DOM order:
  - the sidebar comes first (or last with `side: end`, which also renders it last);
  - the aside comes after the main column;
  - the rail comes first, as a horizontal strip that scrolls inside itself.

  From 769px they are two columns. From 1024px wide and 640px tall, on screens only, the aside and the rail are sticky. Print shows them static and stacked.
- **`visibleWhen` wrappers** are matched as `[data-ak-when]` in layout CSS, never `.ak-when`, because `.ak-when` is the `state` feature's verify marker.

## Contract notes

- **Features.** There are three module features:
  - `layout-options`: grid columns 7–12 and `auto`, grid `gap`/`align`, stack `align`;
  - `layout-tracks`: marker `.ak-grid[data-ak-tracks]`;
  - `semantic-layout`: marker `.ak-sidebar-layout`.

  `grid-item` and the three layouts declare their feature statically. Grid and stack have always been on every page, so they add `layout-options` from their `check`, through `useFeature(node, name)`, and only when a non-default option is used. This is the same node mutation normalize applies for `visibleWhen` (`state`). As a result, existing grids, stacks and pages are byte-identical: `snapshots:check` passes, and `gallery:check` reports only `responsive-layouts.html` (new) and `index.html`.
  - If the controller prefers a definition-level hook, for example `featuresFor(props)` in the frozen core, `useFeature` is the single call site to swap.
- **Diagnostics.**
  - `columns` outside 1–12 or not `auto` is an error.
  - `minItemWidth` without `columns: auto` is a warning.
  - `columns: auto` on a grid with grid items is a warning, and the grid falls back to 3 for the default span.
  - When `start + span - 1 > 12`, it is an error at `.start`, with `details.maxStart`.
  - `grid-item` outside a grid is rejected by the W0 `parents` rule at `.type`.
- **Rail label.** `rail-layout.label` is the rail's accessible name. When it is absent, the name is "Section links" for a nav or "Side rail" for a region (English, like the outline's "On this page").

## Known cross-package test interactions

- `tests/unit/block-modules-registry.test.ts` › "accepts a valid group and appends it after the built-ins" asserts `registry.features` equals the probe feature only. It assumed that no built-in group ships features, which stops being true once any wave 1 package adds one. The one-line fix is `expect(registry.features.at(-1)).toEqual(FEATURE)`. This test is not owned by this package, so it is left unedited.
- `tests/unit/backward-compat.test.ts` iterates every fixture. Per the controller, it is pinned on integration, so any failure caused by the new fixture resolves at merge.
